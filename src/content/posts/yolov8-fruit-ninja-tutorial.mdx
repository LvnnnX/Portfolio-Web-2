## Why this project at all

I wanted a tight, end-to-end computer-vision project that forced me to hold three things in my head at once: detection accuracy, real-time latency, and a closed-loop action layer. Fruit Ninja is an unusually good fit because:

- **Frame rate matters.** A model that's 5 ms slower per frame is a model that misses fruit.
- **Class imbalance is real.** Bombs are rare and catastrophic — a bot that can't tell a watermelon from a bomb is a bot that lasts 10 seconds.
- **The action layer is simple but unforgiving.** Mouse paths must be smooth and *predict* fruit trajectories, not just react to them.

## The capture stack

The first lesson: **don't underestimate frame capture**. PrintScreen-style approaches max out at ~30 FPS on Windows because they go through GDI. If your detector runs at 60 FPS but capture caps at 30, you've wasted half your inference budget.

Solution: [`bettercam`](https://github.com/RootKit-Org/BetterCam) — a thin wrapper around DXGI Desktop Duplication. Sub-millisecond captures, no flicker, no GDI bottleneck.

```python
import bettercam
cam = bettercam.create(output_idx=0, output_color="BGR")
frame = cam.grab()  # < 1 ms typical
```

If `frame is None`, the desktop hasn't changed since the last grab. Handle that explicitly — don't pass `None` into your detector.

## Choosing a YOLOv8 variant

Ultralytics ships YOLOv8 in 5 sizes: `n`, `s`, `m`, `l`, `x`. Each has its own params/FLOPs profile:

| Variant | Params | mAP@0.5 (COCO) | Why you'd pick it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `yolov8n` | 3.2M | 37.3 | Fastest, lowest accuracy |
| `yolov8s` | 11.2M | 44.9 | Sweet spot for many tasks |
| `yolov8m` | 25.9M | 50.2 | Slower, better recall |
| `yolov8l` | 43.7M | 52.9 | Diminishing returns starting |
| `yolov8x` | 68.2M | 53.9 | Most accurate, often overkill |

Cross that with input resolution (320 / 416 / 640) and you have the 13 variants I systematically benchmarked. The takeaway:

> Nano was *nearly* fast enough but lost too much recall on bombs at any resolution. Small at 416 was the actual sweet spot — fast enough on a mid-range GPU, with enough capacity to never confuse a bomb for fruit.

The full benchmark grid lives in the case study; here I'm focusing on *how* to run the comparison.

## Training data, briefly

A few principles that paid off:

1. **Capture at the resolution you'll deploy at.** I trained at 416×416 with mosaic + HSV jitter. Adding 640×640 data made the model heavier but didn't move mAP for *this* domain.
2. **Over-sample bombs.** They're 1-in-15 in natural play but a single miss ends the run. I roughly doubled bomb representation in the training set.
3. **Hold out by *session*, not by frame.** Random frame split leaks easy frames between train and val and inflates mAP. Hold out whole gameplay sessions.

## The action layer

Once the detector returns boxes, you need to swipe through fruit *while skipping bombs*. Two non-obvious tricks:

- **Predict ahead.** Fruit follows a parabola. Compute velocity from the last 2 frames and target a point ~80 ms ahead of where the fruit is *now*. A bot that swipes at *now* always lags.
- **Plan one path, not many.** Group nearby fruits into a single swipe. The game rewards combos, but more importantly fewer mouse moves means lower input-pipeline latency.

```python
# Simplified — see repo for the real planner
def plan_swipe(boxes_now, boxes_prev, dt):
    pairs = match_boxes(boxes_now, boxes_prev)
    targets = [predict_position(p, dt + 0.08) for p in pairs if p.cls != "bomb"]
    return shortest_path(targets)
```

## What broke first

Three things, in order:

1. **Frame skipping under load.** Detector fell behind capture, queue grew, latency exploded. Fix: drop frames at the queue, not at the detector.
2. **Bomb false positives near the screen edge.** Mosaic augmentation had created edge-clipped bombs in training; the model expected to see them everywhere. Fix: regenerate the dataset with `mosaic=0.5` instead of `1.0`.
3. **The dreaded combo miss.** Bot would correctly detect 3 fruits but only swipe through 2 because of an integer overflow in the path planner. Funniest bug. Embarrassing fix.
