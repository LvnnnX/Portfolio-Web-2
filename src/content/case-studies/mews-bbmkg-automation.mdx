## Problem

BBMKG Wilayah III Denpasar is one of Indonesia's regional meteorological agencies. Inside it, the **MEWS division** (Meteorology Early Warning System) produces nine mandatory `.docx` reports every shift — weather summaries, equipment logs, observation handovers — each filled in manually from data scattered across observation tools, spreadsheets, and shift notes.

Two problems compounded:

1. **The same fields were being typed by hand, every shift, every day.** Predictable transcription errors, hours of duplicated effort.
2. **There was no live view of which staff member was on duty.** Schedules were managed by a different division, so the MEWS lead had no quick way to see who was currently in the room.

I joined as a single Universitas Udayana intern in July 2023. The brief: fix both, in the time I had on site.

## Data

Nothing fancy on the data side — that was the point. The MEWS team handed me:

- The nine `.docx` templates that needed to be filled out daily.
- The fields each report expected (shift, observer, observation values, supervisor notes, miscellaneous).
- Their existing on-shift roster format, used to decide who would appear on the monitoring board.

The work was understanding the reports and the workflow, not collecting external data.

## Approach

The internship ran from **10 July 2023 to 10 September 2023**, with the final presentation on **11 September 2023** — about nine weeks end-to-end. I structured it in five stages:

1. **Requirements & scoping.** Sat with MEWS staff to walk through the actual document workflow, not the documented one. Confirmed which nine reports were truly mandatory and what fields each expected.
2. **Tool selection.** Picked **Python + Streamlit** as the stack — Streamlit because it produces a serviceable web UI from a single Python script and matched the team's familiarity with form-style tools, and Python because it has the document libraries (`python-docx`) needed for `.docx` template filling. HTML/CSS layered in only where Streamlit's defaults needed adjustment.
3. **Data integration.** Took each `.docx` template, identified its variable fields, and wired Streamlit form inputs to those fields. Validated that the generated documents were byte-identical in structure to what the team produced by hand.
4. **Build.** Implemented the platform in two surfaces:
    - **Automation page** — staff pick a date, pick one of the nine document types, fill the few fields the system can't derive, and download the rendered `.docx`.
    - **Monitoring page** — shows which staff member is currently on shift, mapped from the existing roster.
5. **Test & training.** Live testing on shift with MEWS staff, presentation + handover on 11 September, runbook for the lead.

## Results

- **9 documents automated end-to-end.** Every mandatory daily MEWS report can now be generated in seconds instead of typed from scratch.
- **Built and shipped in nine weeks.** Solo, on-site, including requirements, build, training, and handover.
- **Operator monitoring layer.** The MEWS lead now has a live view of who is on duty without crossing divisions to ask.
- **Adopted on-shift.** Staff used the platform during user testing and accepted it before handover. (Long-term usage status post-handover wasn't formally tracked, so I'm marking that as unverified rather than claiming it.)

## Lessons learned

- **Walking through the workflow beats reading the spec.** The first week of sitting with MEWS staff surfaced shadow steps that weren't in the original brief — fields filled from a clipboard pinned to the wall, defaults that everyone "knew" but no one wrote down. Skipping that week would have shipped the wrong tool.
- **Boring stack > clever stack.** Streamlit isn't elegant, but it ships a working form-driven web app in days, not weeks. For a solo nine-week internship that's the right trade.
- **Handoff is the project, not a postscript.** A two-month intern building anything operational is a failure mode unless the team can run it after the intern leaves. The runbook took as long to write as some of the document modules.
